### Added
- **2026-11-15 — A register is a chain of revisions, and every date carries its own review and lock state:** A generated draft is no longer a single stored document that later work would have to overwrite. It starts a logical register, and the register points at one accepted revision; every later correction or regeneration is a new revision beside the earlier ones rather than an update to them, so what the office read yesterday still reads the same today. Revisions are numbered within their register instead of across the whole database, because two unrelated registers have no reason to share a sequence. Each revision records its parent revision, the run it came from, what kind of change produced it, the reason given for that change, the seed, the outcome and its position in the register, and the register itself carries the mode and date range it was opened for. Only a draft that completed allocation becomes a revision: an attempt that produced no draft is still stored with its evidence and still reports no revision number, because it has none. `dailyworkreg/infrastructure/sqlite/revision_state.py` holds rows for registers, revisions, review state, locks, entries, decisions and the change audit, and imports nothing from the application layer, so the two layers still cannot import each other. Review is per calendar date and explicit: every date of the revision has a row, including Sundays, General Holidays, leave days and dates carrying nothing at all, because reviewing an empty Sunday is as much part of the job as reviewing a busy Monday. A newly generated revision has every date pending and none reviewed: allocation completing is not a person reading it, and treating it as reviewed would put a reviewer's name on a machine's arithmetic. A date is marked reviewed against a content fingerprint, and a review offered against content the operator is no longer looking at is refused under `review_content_changed_since_it_was_shown` rather than recorded against a row nobody saw. A month is reported reviewed only when every one of its dates is, and that state is derived on read rather than stored, so it cannot disagree with the dates beneath it. Locking is separate from review in both directions: a date can be locked without ever being read, and read without being locked, and the two counts are reported separately. Unlocking a date that is not locked is refused under `date_is_not_locked` so a click that does nothing cannot be mistaken for a click that worked. A correction to a superseded revision is refused under `revision_is_not_current`, because reviewing or locking history would answer a question about a register nobody is working on and an edit there would silently fork the lineage. Review survives into a correction only where the row is provably unchanged: the stored content of each row — its classification, both reason lists, per-asset warnings, its visits by asset and provenance, and its entries by identity with English and Kannada kept apart — is canonicalised and hashed, and a reviewed date is carried forward only when the base revision's fingerprint for that date matches the new one, with the revision it was carried from recorded beside it. A changed row therefore arrives pending, and the review that applied to the previous content is left untouched on the revision where it was actually made. A lock travels with a correction and is enforced: if a locked date's content would change, the correction is refused under `locked_date_content_changed` and the operator is told to unlock it first, so a locked date is preserved exactly rather than approximately. The upgrade is migration 3, which is the first migration in this repository that moves data rather than only shaping it, so a `Migration` may now carry an idempotent Python step run inside the same transaction as its statements. On an existing installation every stored generation keeps its rows, visits, exceptions and attribution: each draft is attached to a register grouped by the mode and date range it was produced for, drafts that shared a register are numbered in revision order, and the newest becomes the current revision. `draft_revision` is rebuilt to scope its uniqueness to the register, because SQLite cannot relax a global unique constraint in place. The surface gained the read and write routes for this capability: the register list, one register with its revisions, one revision, a revision's full review state, reviewing and withdrawing a review, and locking and unlocking a date. No route decides anything; each one translates and returns. The generation response now names the register and revision it produced, and a failed attempt names neither while still being listed in the history. Two existing tests changed because the behaviour they pinned changed: revision numbering is now per register, so two unrelated generations are each revision 1 of their own register rather than revisions 1 and 2 of one, and the drafts-versus-attempts test now asserts that. Added `tests/test_review_and_locks.py` with 20 tests covering register and revision creation, every date present including empty ones, no review on the operator's behalf, the audited review with its fingerprint and note, month state derived from the dates, withdrawal, reviewing a date outside the revision, a stale review refused, locking without reviewing, reviewing without locking, unlocking what is not locked, a lock preserving content, a correction leaving the earlier revision readable, an unchanged row keeping its review and saying where it came from, a changed row not inheriting it, a lock surviving a correction while a change to a locked date is refused, a superseded revision refusing review and lock, an unknown register reported as unknown rather than empty, and a real schema-2 to schema-3 upgrade that gains registers and register-scoped numbering. Added six surface tests for the review state, the review and lock round trips, the stale refusal, the register and revision listings and an unknown register. Recorded a test-count correction from the previous change, which reported 25 new tests and a suite of 505 where the committed test file contains 26 test functions and the suite grew by 26 to 506; the changelog is corrected here rather than by rewriting that commit. The full suite is 532 tests, all passing. ### Fixed
- **2026-11-15 — Every feasibility finding now names the assets it concerns, structurally:** Closed the last place a diagnostic could only be read by a human. Several validator findings identified their constraint, limit and observed value but not which asset, or which set of assets, was responsible, so the only way to recover that identity was to parse the message text, which the documented rule forbids. `Evidence` gained `contributing_assets`, using the representation `Violation` already carries, and the existing `asset_id` and `asset_type` fields are now populated wherever the validator holds reliable asset-specific information: a configured minimum above the maximum, a monthly maximum exceeded, a failed revisit-gap placement and an exhausted per-asset search budget each name their asset and its roster type. Attribution is singular or plural and never invented. A fact about one asset names it in the singular fields; a fact about the combined demand of several assets lists them in `contributing_assets` and leaves the singular fields empty, because picking one asset out of a joint proof would be a claim the arithmetic does not make; a fact about the whole problem, such as a node budget exhausted while packing every asset at once, carries neither and says so in its detail, and an empty attribution is presented as empty. Area-capacity evidence names only the area assets whose demand is counted, excluding DTC and feeder assets that consume none of that cap; combined-capacity and joint-packing evidence name the full demanded set across all three categories, which is the assets still owed visits plus those already holding a committed visit in the month. The roster is never named wholesale. The collection is deduplicated, ordered by asset id and paired with the type from the roster in force, and an id absent from the roster is dropped rather than resolved by guesswork, so every name shown is an asset in that run; `attribute_assets()` is the single place that decides that shape, and both `Violation` and `Evidence` use it so the two cannot drift. The preflight path that converts an invalid committed visit into evidence now carries the violation's contributing set, so an infeasibility caused by a commitment is as diagnosable as a rejected schedule. Appending the field last preserved positional construction of `limit` and `actual`; inserting it earlier would have moved a caller's limit into the wrong field silently. The exception store gained `contributing_assets_json` in schema migration 2 with an empty default, so a row written before attribution existed reads as having none, which is the truth about that row, and decoding is lenient throughout: a missing field decodes to empty or absent and an unrecognised asset type is dropped rather than raised, so a history written by another build stays readable. Nothing is recovered from detail to fill a gap, and the request snapshot schema is untouched because evidence serialisation and request snapshots are separate contracts. The generation response now reports each month's evidence and each exception with all nine structured fields, rendering an enum and an already-stored string identically so the client need not know which it received, and the draft screen prints those values in their own columns without reading any message text. Two defects surfaced while building this and were fixed: a reloaded exception carried its asset type as a raw string while an in-memory one carried the enum, and an older assertion pinned the number of migrations rather than their content, which would have made adding a migration look like a regression. ### Fixed
- **2026-11-15 — Cycle obligations, verification, reconciliation and seed effectiveness corrected:** Fixed six defects in the joint target allocator found in review, without changing the settled scheduling policy. Cycle obligations now carry integer visit counts per asset: the resolver previously built a month's obligations from a set of asset ids, so a DTC owing two visits in one month was recorded as owing one while the month total said two, and four visits were silently reduced to one rather than reported as over the monthly maximum. `verify_cycle()` is now an independent structural verifier rather than a key counter: it checks month identity and ordering against the declared cycle, month indices, roster membership, positive integer obligation values, per-month totals against their obligation sums, per-asset totals against both `cycle_totals` and `visits_per_asset`, scheduled assignments against obligations including multiplicity, and the even-distribution contract; `run_cycle()` verifies a resolution before allocating from it and fails closed under `cycle_resolution_invalid` rather than proceeding. Committed visits are now reconciled across the whole cycle: a fixed visit in any month discharges that asset's cycle obligation even when the resolver assigned a different month, so the asset is no longer allocated a second visit; commitments in months that are not selected for placement, and in months whose allocation fails, are still carried forward as history rather than forgotten; committed visits are preserved unchanged and never duplicated; and commitments exceeding the cycle obligation are reported as `cycle_obligation_over_covered` evidence instead of fabricating or silently dropping visits. The allocation seed is now effective: the asset id was the final tie-breaker, so every seed produced the same schedule, and the earlier test compared only per-asset counts, which could never differ. Placement now derives per-asset and per-date tie keys from the seeded generator and ranks assets by constraint pressure, then remaining count, then scheduling weight, then that key, so alternative seeds reach different valid arrangements while per-asset counts stay fixed. The seed still cannot outrank constraint pressure or change an obligation. Scheduling weights are passed into the allocator through `AllocationRequest.weights`, so the weight model is computed once by `DtcWeights` instead of being re-derived, and a missing weight sorts last. An explicitly empty, duplicated or out-of-cycle month selection is now rejected with `InputError` under stable identifiers rather than producing a successful-looking empty result, which previously reported the worst of no months, that is success, from a run that placed nothing; month selections and fallback sequences are processed chronologically so history is never carried backwards. The multi-month cycle and fallback helpers now state that they support full calendar months only and reject a partial period under `partial_period_unsupported`, rather than implying arbitrary date-range support. The integer rounding rule for a prorated partial month remains undeclared and therefore still unresolved. - **2026-11-15 — Cycle-wide commitment reconciliation made independent of month order:** Fixed the remaining cycle-wide fixed-visit defect in the joint allocator. `run_cycle()` counted committed visits across the cycle for over-coverage reporting but discharged obligations only as its chronological loop reached each commitment, starting from an empty tally. A commitment in a month the loop had not reached yet was therefore invisible while an earlier month was placed. Measured: a DTC owing one visit in a three-month cycle, with the resolver assigning that visit to November and a committed visit locked in December, produced **two** visits for an asset owing one and reported no over-coverage, because the over-coverage calculation counted commitments but not generated visits. The same blindness affected the revisit gap: the November allocation could take a date within seven days of a December commitment, December then failed to place, and the cycle was reported infeasible even when another valid November date existed. Measured on a two-visit cycle with a commitment on 2 December: November accepted 30 November, December was rejected, and the cycle came out infeasible; it now places November on 2 November and the cycle is feasible. Fixed visits are now inventoried and reconciled against the whole declared cycle before any month is allocated. Each committed DTC visit consumes exactly one obligation unit — charged to the month it falls in when that month still owes a unit, otherwise to the earliest month that does — so newly allocated visits plus committed visits equal the declared obligation for every DTC, and a whole-cycle commitment count is never subtracted independently from each month, which would over-subtract an asset owing units in two months. Every committed visit outside the month being placed is supplied to the validator as a new third input category, gap blockers, which constrain the seven-calendar-day interval in both directions while consuming no capacity and no obligation in that month; a commitment is never supplied as both the month's fixed visit and a blocker, so it is never counted twice, and the allocator does not duplicate the validator's gap arithmetic. Commitments are validated wherever they fall, including months that are not selected for placement, through a new `validate_committed_visits()` that reuses the same hard-constraint path per calendar month rather than reimplementing it; a mapping for a month outside the cycle, a visit filed under a month that does not contain its date, and a commitment failing the hard constraints all raise `InputError` under stable identifiers instead of being silently accepted as a discharge. This immediately caught a defect in this repository's own test data, which had used a Saturday as a working date in an unselected month. Cycle accounting is restricted to DTC cycle members: committed area and feeder visits are preserved, consume combined and area daily capacity and constrain gaps, but are never compared with the cycle's per-visit obligation and never produce DTC over-coverage evidence. The declared `CycleResolution` is now distinguished from the effective obligations a month is allocated against (`MonthPlan.declared_obligations` versus `MonthPlan.obligations`), and the inventoried commitments are reported on `MultiMonthResult.committed_visits`. Added `tests/test_cycle_commitments.py` with 33 tests covering the duplicate-visit defect, the future-gap constraint, a different earlier date being chosen, commitments in unselected months, over-coverage preservation, area and feeder exclusion from cycle accounting, and every rejection path. The full suite is 274 tests, all passing, with all 241 preceding tests retained; the allocator harness gained a future-commitment section and the validator soundness harness is unchanged.
