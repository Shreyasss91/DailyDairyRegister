## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **2026-11-15 — Committed visits are validated on every feasibility path:** Fixed a false-FEASIBLE path in the field-visit feasibility assessment. The assessment subtracted what committed in-period visits already discharged and, when no obligation remained to place, returned FEASIBLE immediately without establishing that those committed visits formed a valid schedule. The concrete-schedule validator rejected the same input, so one question had two answers and an invalid month could be recorded as feasible. Hard-constraint evaluation now lives in one shared path used by both `validate_schedule` and `assess_feasibility`, and the assessment runs it as preflight before every return that could be FEASIBLE, including the no-remaining-obligations branch. An invalid committed schedule now yields INFEASIBLE with structured evidence naming the constraint, asset, offending dates, limit and observed value. `Evidence` gained `asset_id`, `asset_type`, `visit_date` and `offending_dates`. A committed visit to a known but inactive asset is now rejected by both paths under the new `fixed_visit_on_inactive_asset` constraint; previously it was accepted by validation and by assessment merely because the asset existed in the roster. Obligation counts now refuse boolean values, which Python would otherwise accept as integers and treat as a count of one. Fixed-visit accounting is unchanged: committed visits consume combined and area daily capacity, count once toward the per-asset monthly maximum, discharge part of their obligation, constrain placements through the seven-calendar-day interval and remain in the returned witness without duplication. Added `tests/test_fixed_visit_validation.py` with 20 tests and recorded the corrected contract in §10.8 of the requirements baseline. The three-outcome model and all approved owner policies are unchanged. No allocator, rotation, cycle-window resolution, scheduler, register generator, API, schema or UI was implemented and no solver dependency was added.
- **2026-11-15 — Validator contract hardened for the upcoming allocator:** Corrected the field-visit validator contract before any target allocator consumes it. Malformed obligation inputs are now rejected explicitly instead of being silently dropped: duplicate roster asset ids, obligation or cycle-obligation or limit-override keys naming an unknown asset, and negative or non-integer counts all raise a domain `InputError` carrying a stable constraint identifier. A positive obligation or configured positive minimum attached to an inactive asset is likewise an input error rather than a skipped entry, because skipping it reported a month as feasible while owed work was never scheduled. A malformed input is kept distinct from a well-formed but mathematically infeasible obligation set. Assessment is now defined for one calendar month: `DatePeriod` rejects a period spanning two months, which previously applied a single month's per-asset maximum to both months and hid per-asset overrun, while partial months within one calendar month remain valid and cycle obligations are resolved by the caller into the monthly portion. Added a shared `ObligationSpec` resolved once by `resolve_obligations` and consumed by both proposed-schedule validation and feasibility assessment, so the obligation-aware mode now honours explicit required counts and cycle obligations as well as configured minimums, resolving to the largest source and never treating a monthly maximum as an obligation. Added an explicit `fixed` input for visits already committed inside the month, separate from prior history: committed visits consume combined and area daily capacity, count toward the per-asset monthly maximum, discharge part of the obligation, constrain new placements through the seven-calendar-day interval, and are returned in the witness so a feasible result describes the whole month. Committed visits outside the month or off an eligible working date are reported rather than discarded. `Violation` now carries `offending_dates` (all dates involved, including every date counted by a monthly-maximum violation), `contributing_assets` with their types for daily-capacity violations, plus limit and actual values, so no caller parses message text; `conflicting_dates` remains as an alias. Added `tests/test_validator_contract.py` with 34 tests covering every correction, and recorded the enforced input contract as §10.8 of the requirements baseline. A reproducible brute-force soundness harness is kept locally under `temp/dailyworkreg/`. No allocator, weighted rotation, scheduler, generator, API, schema or UI was implemented and no solver dependency was added.
- **2026-11-15 — Date-level field-visit feasibility validator implemented:** Added the first scheduling-domain capability as pure Python under `dailyworkreg/domain/validator.py`, with tests in `tests/test_feasibility_validator.py`. It implements the hard constraints recorded in §10.1 to §10.7 and separates two operations that must not be conflated. Proposed-schedule validation is exact and reports every violation with a stable constraint identifier, the asset id and type, the conflicting dates, the expected limit and the actual value. Target-obligation feasibility assessment returns FEASIBLE only with a re-validatable witness, INFEASIBLE only on a proof (configuration contradiction, obligation above the effective maximum, failed aggregate necessary condition, an asset with no gap-respecting placement, or a completed exhaustive search), and INDETERMINATE when the declared search budget is exhausted first. The module has no FastAPI, SQLite or presentation dependency, keeps dates as date values, and is deterministic and order-independent. Tests cover all §10.6 vectors plus calendar overrides, ineligible holiday and leave dates, cross-month and cross-cycle gaps, mixed-category daily packing, per-asset minimums enforced only in obligation mode, a configured minimum above the maximum, determinism, and two soundness sweeps that cross-check verdicts and witnesses against exhaustive brute force. Added §10.7 to the requirements baseline recording the three-outcome contract, its proof conditions, the fact that greedy placement cannot establish infeasibility, and the cost boundary at which a large roster returns INDETERMINATE. No scheduler, target recommender, register generator, API, schema or UI was implemented.
- **2026-11-15 — Corrected field-visit feasibility model and deterministic test vectors:** Extended §10 of the requirements baseline with a new §10.6 recording the corrected mathematical model for field-visit feasibility. It defines the notation `W` (eligible working dates), `C` (combined daily capacity), `A`, `D`, `F` (required area, DTC and feeder visits); states the two necessary aggregate conditions `A <= 2 * W` and `A + D + F <= C * W` and labels them necessary but not sufficient; separates the area-specific daily maximum of 2 from the combined daily cap of 3; records that area, DTC and feeder visits may share a working date so category day counts must not be added without a non-overlap proof; restates the 7-calendar-day revisit interval with the `(v - 1) * 7` minimum calendar span and the rule that month and cycle boundaries do not reset the interval; explains that a per-asset monthly maximum of 3 is an upper bound and not a visit obligation; records a conditional 16-area example (48 required visits against a 46 maximum at 23 working days, hence infeasible) marked explicitly as illustrative rather than an approved target; and adds a 22-row deterministic test-vector table covering calendar-gap boundaries, weekend and cross-month/cross-cycle gaps, per-asset monthly caps, area and combined daily caps, mixed-category packing, aggregate necessary conditions, the 113-DTC quarterly cycle and its 38/38/37 allocation, and deterministic versus alternative regeneration. §16 item 1 was extended to state that these test vectors define the validation basis for the joint allocator, which remains unimplemented and unvalidated. This is a requirements and design change only; no scheduler, application code, schema or dependency was added, and no runtime capability is claimed.
- **2026-11-15 — DTC coverage-cycle and per-asset limit policy:** Updated §10 and §16 of the requirements baseline to record an owner-declared DTC coverage cycle as the primary target mode, a capacity-bounded, weight-informed and fair-rotation monthly fallback when no cycle is declared, and an initial default maximum of 3 visits per asset per calendar month for areas, DTCs and feeders. Clarified that this monthly per-asset cap is independent of the 3-visits-per-working-day combined cap, and preserved the settled 7-calendar-day revisit interval across cycle boundaries. Recorded that area/feeder full-roster monthly coverage is not approved and that the candidate mathematical bound still requires date-level proof and deterministic validation. Requirements/design only; no scheduler or application behavior was implemented.
